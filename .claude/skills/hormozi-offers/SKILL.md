# Hormozi Offers — $100M Offer Framework

Built from *$100M Offers* and *$100M Leads* by Alex Hormozi. The single most actionable offer framework in modern direct response. Use it to turn any product into a Grand Slam Offer.

## Part 1 — The Value Equation (the core)

Every offer's perceived value is determined by one equation:

```
                  Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
    Value   =   ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                          Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice
```

**To increase perceived value, you do ONE of four things:**

1. **Increase Dream Outcome** (make the promise bigger/more vivid/more specific)
2. **Increase Perceived Likelihood** (reduce risk, show proof, add guarantees)
3. **Decrease Time Delay** (make results faster, even if not actually faster)
4. **Decrease Effort & Sacrifice** (done-for-them > done-with-you > DIY)

**Most people compete on price. Grand Slam Offers compete on the Value Equation.** Price only matters when value is unclear. Make value obvious and price becomes almost irrelevant.

### Working each lever

#### Dream Outcome
- **Bad**: "Save time on marketing"
- **Good**: "Fill your calendar with 10 qualified sales calls in the next 30 days"
- Be specific, measurable, emotional, and concrete.
- Use the prospect's exact words from interviews (see `mom-test` skill).
- Show the *status* result as well as the *functional* result ("look like the smartest founder in your industry").

#### Perceived Likelihood
- **Case studies with names + numbers + photos**
- **Third-party validation** (press, credentials, certifications)
- **Transparency** (show the process, not just the result)
- **Guarantees** (see Part 4)
- **Risk reversal** ("you only pay if it works")

#### Time Delay
- **First result fast** (even a small win in the first 24 hours)
- **Milestones** so the path feels short ("you'll see X by day 3, Y by day 7, Z by day 30")
- **Reframe** long processes as "during the 12 weeks you'll feel X, Y, Z change weekly"
- **Immediate delivery** of something (even just a course module or PDF) reduces perceived time-to-value

#### Effort & Sacrifice
- **Done-for-them > done-with-you > DIY**
- Remove decisions ("we handle X so you don't")
- Remove learning curve ("no tech skills needed")
- Remove time commitment ("15 minutes per day")
- Remove emotional cost ("no cold calls, no posting")
- Remove physical/social discomfort

**Rule of thumb**: Every sentence of your sales page should be improving one of these four variables.

## Part 2 — The Grand Slam Offer structure

A Grand Slam Offer has 5 components, stacked:

```
1. Core offer              (the thing you sell)
+ 2. Bonuses               (unique, stackable value)
+ 3. Scarcity              (real, not fake)
+ 4. Urgency               (deadline)
+ 5. Guarantee             (risk reversal)
+ 6. Naming                (makes it memorable)
= Grand Slam Offer
```

Each part multiplies the whole. Remove any one and the offer weakens disproportionately.

## Part 3 — Offer stacking (bonuses done right)

The core offer is often ~$X. You add bonuses that each have clear standalone value, so the total perceived value becomes 5-10× the price.

### Rules for bonuses
1. **Each bonus solves a specific objection** the prospect has
   - "I don't have time" → "Done-for-you [X] template"
   - "I can't write copy" → "100 swipe-file headlines"
   - "I don't know if it'll work for my niche" → "50 case studies in your niche"
2. **Each bonus has a concrete value** ("$497 value") — this is not made up, it's what you'd charge if sold separately
3. **Each bonus is named** like a product ("The 7-Figure Email Swipe File") not a feature ("email templates")
4. **Each bonus has a reason for being free** ("because X")
5. **Name the bonus, describe it, show the price, add it to the stack**

### Example stack
```
The Core Offer: 12-week coaching program         ($5,000 value)
Bonus 1: Weekly 1:1 calls                        ($2,000 value)
Bonus 2: The Grand Slam Offer template           (  $497 value)
Bonus 3: Done-for-you email sequences            (  $997 value)
Bonus 4: Private community + hot-seat coaching   ($1,500 value)
Bonus 5: 60-day action plan                      (  $297 value)
Bonus 6: Lifetime updates                        (  $997 value)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Total value:                                    $11,288
Today:                                           $1,997
```

**The gap between stack value and price is the offer's emotional weight.**

## Part 4 — Guarantees (the four types)

### 1. Unconditional
"100% money back, no questions asked, anytime in the next 30 days."
- Highest conversion, highest refund rate
- Works when your product is genuinely great and price is moderate

### 2. Conditional ("results-based")
"If you don't get [specific measurable result] in [timeframe], I'll refund you + give you $500."
- Lower refund rate (people fail to hit the condition)
- Higher emotional confidence
- The condition must be objective and measurable

### 3. Anti-guarantee
"All sales are final. Here's why: [reason, usually about commitment]."
- Only works when you can frame the no-guarantee as a *benefit* to the buyer (serious people only, no tire-kickers)
- Counterintuitively converts well in high-ticket with right framing

### 4. Implied guarantee
"We're so confident, we'll keep working with you until you get [result]."
- Extends service rather than returning money
- Great for SaaS, agencies, coaches

### The "better-than-money-back" guarantee
Combine types: "If you don't get [result], I'll refund 100% AND pay you $500 for your time." Signals such certainty that prospect feels obligated to believe.

## Part 5 — Scarcity (must be real)

Scarcity multiplies desire. But **fake scarcity destroys trust forever**. Real scarcity sources:

1. **Supply cap** — "I only work with 10 clients per quarter"
2. **Quantity cap** — "Only 50 seats total for this cohort"
3. **Bonus cap** — "The first 20 buyers get [extra bonus X]"
4. **Access cap** — "Office hours limited to [N] per week"
5. **Cohort/batch** — "Next cohort starts [date]. After that, closed for 3 months."

**Never fake countdown timers or "only 3 left" numbers on inventory that isn't real.** One exposure burns the brand for life.

## Part 6 — Urgency (time-bound pressure)

Urgency is time; scarcity is supply. Real urgency sources:

1. **Hard deadline** — "Cohort closes Friday midnight"
2. **Price increase** — "Price goes from $997 → $1,497 on X date"
3. **Bonus expiry** — "This bonus stack disappears on X date"
4. **Cohort start** — "Miss this one, wait 3 months for the next"
5. **External event tied deadline** — "Before Apple's Q3 launch" / "Before tax season"

**Rule**: Every offer page needs both scarcity AND urgency. Together they move fence-sitters.

## Part 7 — Naming formula (M.A.G.I.C.)

From $100M Offers: names convert. A great offer name does five things:

**M** — Makes a **M**agnetic reason (why they need it now)
**A** — Announces the **A**vatar (who it's for)
**G** — **G**ives a time frame (how long to result)
**I** — **I**dentifies the goal (what they get)
**C** — **C**ontainer word ("method", "blueprint", "system", "cheat-code")

### Examples
- "The **30-Day Qualified Lead Blueprint** for Coaches Who Hate Cold DMs"
- "The **6-Week Gold Scalping Method** for MT5 Traders Tired of Blowing Accounts"
- "The **90-Day Founder Sleep System** for SaaS Operators Running on 5 Hours"

### Avoid
- Generic names ("Marketing Course")
- Clever-but-vague names ("The Flow State")
- Names that don't fit the Value Equation

## Part 8 — Pricing philosophy

Three price principles from Hormozi:

1. **Charge more.** Most people under-price by 3-10×. The remedy is not a discount — it's a better offer.
2. **Price = commitment.** People value what they pay for. Free users are the hardest to help.
3. **Price anchors the value.** A $10k offer instantly signals more value than a $100 offer, even before any proof.

### The "10x test"
Is the offer worth **10× the price**? If yes, the price is right or too low. If no, fix the offer first (don't lower the price).

### The "take it away" test
If you took away 3 bonuses and kept the core, would prospects still buy at the same price? If yes, your core is strong. If no, your core is weak and bonuses are carrying the offer — rebuild.

## Part 9 — Objection neutralization

Before someone buys, 4 objections fire in their head:
1. **Not enough time** → time-saving bonus, DFY option, fast-start
2. **Not enough money** → payment plan, guarantee, value stack, ROI case
3. **Won't work for me** → industry-specific case studies, "people like you" proof
4. **Won't work, period** → 3rd-party validation, founders' credibility, guarantee

**Every Grand Slam Offer sales page handles all four before they form**. Put them in the order above, with a section each.

## Part 10 — The $100M Leads follow-up

From the second book — once your offer is Grand Slam, you apply the **Core Four** lead sources:
1. **Warm outreach** (your existing contacts)
2. **Content** (posting free value publicly)
3. **Paid ads**
4. **Cold outreach**

And you layer:
- **Lead Magnet** (free thing that demonstrates the expensive thing works)
- **Paid ad → landing page → offer → upsell → retention**

The Grand Slam Offer is the engine. Leads are the fuel. Both are needed.

## Audit checklist

Use this when reviewing any existing offer:

- [ ] Is the Dream Outcome specific, measurable, and emotional?
- [ ] Is Perceived Likelihood high (proof, guarantees, process transparency)?
- [ ] Is Time Delay minimized or reframed?
- [ ] Is Effort & Sacrifice minimized (DFY > DWY > DIY)?
- [ ] Are there 4-6 bonuses, each solving a specific objection?
- [ ] Does each bonus have a standalone value, name, and reason to be free?
- [ ] Is total stack value ≥ 10× the price?
- [ ] Is there a real, specific guarantee?
- [ ] Is there real scarcity (not fake)?
- [ ] Is there a time-bound deadline?
- [ ] Does the offer have a MAGIC name?
- [ ] Are all 4 objections handled in the copy?
- [ ] Would the buyer feel stupid saying no?

If 10+ boxes are ticked, you have a Grand Slam Offer. <10 = rebuild.
