# Subconscious Copywriting — Speak to the Deeper Mind

Based on Milton Erickson's hypnotic language patterns (the "Milton Model"), NLP language research, and classical rhetoric. This is how top copywriters and persuaders bypass the analytical mind and speak directly to the decision-making unconscious.

## The two minds you're writing to

Every reader has two minds processing your copy simultaneously:

1. **Conscious/critical mind** — slow, skeptical, counter-arguing, looking for reasons to say no. This is what reads headlines and looks for red flags.
2. **Unconscious/emotional mind** — fast, associative, feeling-based, running pattern-matching on tone/rhythm/images/story. This is what actually makes the buying decision.

Subconscious copywriting **satisfies the critical mind just enough to get through**, while **speaking the real message to the unconscious**. You don't hide — you layer.

## Part 1 — The Milton Model (hypnotic language patterns)

Milton Erickson was the most influential hypnotherapist of the 20th century. Bandler and Grinder modeled his language and called it the Milton Model. Every world-class copywriter uses these patterns, usually unknowingly.

### 1. Mind-reads (be careful, be vague)
> "I know you're wondering if this is right for you."
> "You're probably thinking about how much time you've wasted on things that didn't work."

The brain completes the thought and **agrees with itself**, then credits you with understanding.

### 2. Lost performatives (deleted source)
> "It's widely known that X."
> "Many people have found that Y."

No source = no one to argue with. The brain accepts it as background fact.

### 3. Cause-effect (implied)
> "As you read these words, you will start to see why this works."
> "Because you've read this far, you already understand the opportunity."

**"As" + "while" + "when"** create pseudo-causal links. The brain accepts the link because it's grammatically invisible.

### 4. Mind-complex equivalence
> "Reading this means you're ready for change."
> "Being on this page is proof you're serious about your business."

Equates two things that aren't logically connected. Hard to counter because there's no argument to refute.

### 5. Presuppositions (the big one)
Presuppositions bury claims inside structure. The brain has to **accept** them to process the sentence:

- **Time presupposition**: "Before you buy, let me show you…" (presupposes buying)
- **Awareness**: "Have you noticed how much easier this is?" (presupposes it's easier)
- **Adjective**: "The other powerful benefit is…" (presupposes there already was a powerful one)
- **Ordinal**: "The first reason you'll love this…" (presupposes you'll love it)
- **Awareness**: "You can see right now how this works for you" (presupposes it works)
- **Complex adjective**: "Another reason people choose us…" (presupposes people choose us)
- **Exclusive-or**: "Do you want to start with X or Y?" (presupposes you start)
- **Repetitive**: "The more you read, the more interested you become." (presupposes interest building)

**Power rule**: The real message goes in the presupposition, not the assertion. The conscious mind argues about the assertion; the presupposition slips past.

### 6. Embedded commands
Hidden commands inside normal sentences. The unconscious picks them up as direct instructions:

> "You don't have to **buy this now** to feel what it would be like."
> "People **join today** for different reasons."
> "I'm not going to tell you to **click the button** — that's your choice."

**Rules:**
- The command is a complete imperative (buy this, click now, sign up)
- Marked subtly in spoken form with a voice tonal shift; in writing, with italics, **bold**, or punctuation
- Wrapped in a non-command sentence so the conscious mind processes the container, not the contents

### 7. Universal quantifiers (deliberately vague)
> "Everyone is realizing this."
> "Nobody wants to be left behind."
> "All successful founders eventually learn this."

The brain can't disprove "everyone" easily, so it accepts and moves on.

### 8. Tag questions
> "This makes sense, doesn't it?"
> "You want this, don't you?"

Forces the mind into a micro-yes. Chaining micro-yeses builds a momentum of agreement.

### 9. Double binds
> "Do you want to start now or after you finish reading this page?"
> "Would you like the monthly or annual plan?"

Both options assume the main decision is already made. Conscious mind picks between them; unconscious already agreed to the frame.

### 10. Truisms chained to new claims
> "It's Saturday morning. You're reading this. You probably have a coffee. And you already know that most marketing advice online is repackaged nonsense."

Three undeniable facts, then a suggestion. The brain agrees with the easy truisms, then rides that momentum into agreeing with the suggestion.

## Part 2 — Sensory language (VAKOG)

People process information in five sensory channels. Great copy hits all of them because different readers prefer different channels.

| Channel | Words | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Visual** | see, picture, clear, bright, imagine, visualize, perspective | "**Picture** yourself **clear**ly **seeing** the opportunity" |
| **Auditory** | hear, sounds, tell, ring true, click, say, listen | "This will **click** when you **hear** the real story" |
| **Kinesthetic** | feel, grasp, touch, heavy, solid, warm, comfortable, pressure | "You can **feel** how **solid** this is" |
| **Olfactory** | smell, fresh, stale, fragrant, scent | "Something **smells** off about that promise" |
| **Gustatory** | taste, bitter, sweet, savor, bite | "**Sweet** victory after all that hard work" |

**Rule**: mix all five in long-form copy. Weight toward visual (most dominant) and kinesthetic (closest to emotion).

## Part 3 — Story architecture (speaks to the unconscious)

The unconscious mind is pattern-matched to story shape before humans had writing. Every great sales letter is secretly a story.

### The Hero's Journey (condensed for copy)

1. **Ordinary world** — "I used to be just like you, stuck in X."
2. **Call to adventure** — "Then I saw/heard/discovered Y."
3. **Refusal** — "At first I didn't believe it."
4. **Mentor** — "Until I met/read/learned from Z."
5. **Threshold crossing** — "I decided to try it."
6. **Tests** — "It was hard. I failed at A, B, C."
7. **Breakthrough** — "Then I realized the one thing that changed everything."
8. **Return with the elixir** — "Now I want to give this to you."

Every Hormozi pitch, every Brunson webinar, every great VSL uses this structure. Because the unconscious has been trained on it for 100,000 years.

### The StoryBrand / Donald Miller shortcut

1. A hero (the reader)
2. has a problem
3. and meets a guide (you)
4. who gives them a plan
5. and calls them to action,
6. that helps them avoid failure
7. and ends in success.

This is the safest sales-story skeleton for landing pages. `storybrand-messaging` skill has the full application.

## Part 4 — Rhythm, cadence, and sound

The unconscious responds to **prosody** — rhythm and sound — more than semantics.

### Sentence length variation
> "I was stuck. I had tried everything. Every framework, every guru, every morning routine, every so-called secret that promised results but only delivered the same empty feeling at the end of another wasted month. Then one thing changed. Everything."

Short → long → short → one word. Like music. The unconscious feels the shape.

### Rule of three
Three is the shortest list that feels complete. "Life, liberty, pursuit of happiness." "Blood, sweat, tears." "Faster, better, cheaper." Hammer this pattern.

### Alliteration and assonance
"Clear, concise, compelling." "Built to build businesses." The ear likes repeated sounds; the unconscious codes it as "well-made".

### Parallel structure
> "I used to wake up tired, go to bed tired, and wonder why nothing worked. I now wake up clear, go to bed satisfied, and know why everything works."

Parallel structure mirrors change. The reader feels the transformation.

### The power pause
Single-sentence paragraphs.

Like this.

They force the reader to breathe.

Rhythm = emotion = sale.

## Part 5 — Metaphor (the unconscious speaks in metaphor)

Metaphors bypass logical evaluation. "My business is bleeding money" is not literally true but the brain **feels** it.

**Rules for copy metaphors:**
1. **Use sensory metaphors, not abstract** — "stuck in a maze" > "confused"
2. **Use journey metaphors for transformation** — path, crossroads, destination, climb
3. **Use object metaphors for solutions** — key, lever, bridge, shortcut, blueprint
4. **Mix in stories** so the metaphor is lived, not explained
5. **Never explain the metaphor** — if you have to, it's broken

### Extended metaphor trick

Pick one metaphor and extend it through the entire copy. Example: "Your business is a garden. Most founders plant too many seeds in bad soil. Today I'll show you how to prepare the soil, pick the right seeds, and harvest twice as much with half the water."

The reader's unconscious tracks one consistent image = deeper engagement.

## Part 6 — Pacing and leading

Milton's most powerful trick: agree with what the reader is already experiencing (**pace**), then subtly introduce what you want them to experience next (**lead**).

```
You've been reading for a few minutes now [pace],
you're probably sitting comfortably [pace],
and as your eyes move down this page [pace],
you'll start to notice how the right words can change how you see things [LEAD].
```

Three true pacing statements + one leading suggestion. The brain accepts the pattern and follows the lead.

**Copy move**: Start every paid landing page with 3-5 lines pacing the reader's current state (pain, frustration, hope), then lead into your solution as the next natural thing.

## Part 7 — Negation (what NOT to do with NOT)

The unconscious mind doesn't process negation. "Don't think of a pink elephant" makes you think of a pink elephant.

**Wrong**: "You won't lose money."
**Right**: "You'll keep every dollar."

**Wrong**: "Don't worry about failure."
**Right**: "You're going to succeed."

**Deliberate negation trick** (advanced): "Don't buy this yet. Not until you've read every word." The unconscious strips the "don't" and hears "buy this." The conscious mind thinks you're being restrained.

## Part 8 — Trance induction in long-form copy

A long VSL or sales letter is structurally a light trance induction:

1. **Fixation** — pull attention with a pattern-interrupt headline
2. **Pacing** — 3-5 statements that match reader's experience
3. **Leading** — suggest the new frame
4. **Story** — shift into narrative (unconscious takes over)
5. **Revelation** — the breakthrough insight (peak of the trance)
6. **Reframe** — their old world is small, new world is big
7. **Call to action** — the command while the trance is warm

Read any Halbert, Kennedy, or Hormozi long-form pitch. The structure is always this.

## Part 9 — Words that bypass critical thinking

Research-validated high-impact words:

- **"You"** — most powerful single word in copy
- **"Because"** — unlocks compliance even with weak reasons
- **"Free"** — disproportionate emotional pull (vs. even $0.01)
- **"New"** — triggers attention and curiosity
- **"Proven"** — softens skepticism
- **"Imagine"** — activates mental simulation (brain doesn't separate real from imagined)
- **"Easy"** — reduces perceived effort
- **"Now"** — collapses time delay
- **"Guaranteed"** — reduces risk perception
- **"Your [name]"** — personalization amplifies everything

## Part 10 — What breaks the spell

Immediate trance-breakers — avoid at all costs:

- **Logical inconsistency** the reader catches (critical mind wakes up)
- **Hype that feels fake** (triggers skepticism cascade)
- **Sudden tonal shift** from warm to corporate
- **Demand before desire** (asking for sale before rapport is built)
- **Too many CTAs in one section** (reader feels manipulated)
- **Stock photos of fake happy people** (uncanny valley)
- **Perfection with no flaws** (no humans are perfect; perfect = fake)

## Ethics and calibration

Same rules as `persuasion-deep`: these techniques work in both directions. Use them to help people decide for things they genuinely want. Manipulating someone into harm ends careers and breaks trust ecosystems.

**Rule of thumb**: If you'd be comfortable showing the customer the technique name you used and why, it's ethical. If you'd hide it from them, reconsider.
