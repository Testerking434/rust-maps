# Schwartz Awareness Levels — The Prospect's Head

From *Breakthrough Advertising* (Eugene Schwartz, 1966 — still the most sophisticated copywriting book ever written). Every marketer who thinks about audience must pass through Schwartz's framework first.

**The one question that determines everything:** *What does your prospect already know, and where is their head right now?*

Write the wrong hook for the wrong awareness level and you lose the sale before they read the second line. Write the right hook for the right level and the copy practically writes itself.

## Part 1 — The 5 Levels of Awareness

Schwartz identified 5 distinct mental states prospects can be in, from "knows nothing" to "ready to buy now". Each requires completely different copy.

### Level 1 — Unaware
**They don't know they have a problem.**

- Doesn't know the problem exists
- Doesn't know they're suffering from it
- Doesn't use language around it
- Can't be sold a solution because there's no felt problem

**What works:** Identification + story. Start with a character/scene they recognize, **make them feel the symptom**, then slowly reveal the problem has a name.

**Hook patterns:**
- "I was sitting in my car at 6pm on a Tuesday, and something felt wrong…"
- "Most founders don't realize this, but there's a silent tax on your time that…"
- Story-driven entry; no direct offer

**Copy length:** Long. You must build the problem from scratch.

**Channels that work:** Long-form content, YouTube videos, newsletters, personal stories on social.

**Don't:** Jump straight to offer. Don't name your product. Don't mention benefits. They don't have a problem yet; you're installing one.

### Level 2 — Problem-Aware
**They know they have a problem. They don't know solutions exist.**

- Feels the pain
- Searches for information about the pain
- Doesn't know there's a category of solution
- Uses pain-language ("I'm exhausted", "my funnel doesn't convert", "my EAs keep blowing up")

**What works:** Agitate the problem, reveal it has a name and a cause, then introduce that **there is a category of solution** (without naming yours yet).

**Hook patterns:**
- "If you're [feeling X], it's not your fault. It's because of [Y]."
- "The 3 reasons your [problem] keeps happening — and what's actually causing it"
- "Why your [problem] never gets better, no matter how hard you try"

**Copy length:** Medium to long. Problem → cause → category of solution → your product.

**Channels:** Search ads (people searching problem terms), Facebook/IG ads with pain hooks, Reddit threads, Google.

**Don't:** Lead with your product. Don't assume they know solutions. They'll reject your offer if they don't first accept there's a type of fix.

### Level 3 — Solution-Aware
**They know solutions exist. They don't know about yours. Or they know yours exists but don't think it's the best.**

- Compares options
- Reads reviews
- Uses category-language ("Python backtesting frameworks", "CRO tools", "AI copywriters")
- Has some resistance — "I've tried stuff before"

**What works:** Differentiation + unique mechanism. Show that all existing solutions **miss something**, and you have the one thing they don't.

**Hook patterns:**
- "Why every [category solution] fails for [specific audience] — and the one different approach that works"
- "I tried [5 competitors]. Here's what none of them do."
- "The missing ingredient in [category]"

**Copy length:** Medium. Focus on the **unique mechanism** — the one thing you do differently.

**Channels:** Comparison pages, "X vs Y" content, landing pages with prospect searching category terms, retargeting ads.

**Don't:** Just list features. At this level, prospect is cynical. You must have a **unique mechanism** (the "secret sauce" that makes your solution different at a structural level).

**Key concept — Unique Mechanism**: the specific reason *your* solution works differently. Examples:
- "We don't use indicators. We read the volume profile directly." (trading)
- "No dieting. No willpower. We rewire your glucose response." (fitness)
- "We don't optimize your funnel. We rebuild the offer at the Value Equation level." (marketing)

Unique Mechanism is the single biggest lever at Level 3.

### Level 4 — Product-Aware
**They know your product exists. They're on the fence.**

- Been on your site before, read emails, seen ads
- Knows roughly what you do
- Not yet convinced it works *for them*
- Has specific objections (price, fit, timing, risk)

**What works:** Remove specific objections. Social proof from **people like them**. Guarantees. Risk reversal. Stack bonuses. Direct offer.

**Hook patterns:**
- "Still on the fence about [Product]? Here's the case you haven't seen."
- "What [Product] actually does for [specific situation]"
- "The 3 reasons most [audience] say no to [Product] — and why they're wrong"

**Copy length:** Shorter. They don't need problem-setup. They need objection-demolition.

**Channels:** Email nurture, retargeting ads, comparison pages, case studies, testimonials page, FAQ page.

**Don't:** Re-explain the problem. Re-explain the category. They know. Move directly to objection + proof + offer.

### Level 5 — Most Aware
**They know your product. They like it. They just haven't bought yet.**

- On your email list
- Opens your emails
- Knows the price, knows the value
- Needs a reason **now** — a deal, a deadline, a bonus, a new angle

**What works:** Offer. Short. Specific. Scarcity. Urgency. Bonus.

**Hook patterns:**
- "[Product] — 40% off until Friday"
- "I'm closing the program this week. Here's the bonus stack for this cohort only."
- "New bonus: [X]. Added today, gone Sunday."

**Copy length:** Short. One email. One offer. One CTA.

**Channels:** Email blast, SMS, retargeting ads showing the offer, podcast announcements to warm audience.

**Don't:** Write a 5000-word sales letter. They've already read it. Give them the reason to act now.

## Part 2 — The 5 Market Sophistication Stages

Awareness is about the **prospect's knowledge**. Sophistication is about the **market's history** — how many times this market has already heard similar claims. Schwartz's insight: the same claim that works beautifully in a fresh market fails in a saturated one.

### Stage 1 — Fresh market
The first entrant in a category. The prospect has never heard any claim here.

**What works:** State the claim directly.
- "Lose 20 pounds in 30 days"
- "Make money while you sleep"
- "The first AI that writes your emails for you"

### Stage 2 — Market has heard the claim; second entrant
A competitor has used the same direct claim. Now you need to **enlarge the claim**.

**What works:** Bigger promise or bigger proof.
- "Lose 30 pounds in 30 days — twice as fast as the leading method"
- "The AI that writes 10x more emails than [competitor], for a fraction of the price"

### Stage 3 — Market is skeptical of direct claims; need a mechanism
The market has heard big claims so many times they stopped believing. Now you need to introduce **how it works** — the mechanism.

**What works:** Tell them *why* it works, not just *that* it works.
- "Lose weight by resetting your insulin response"
- "Write emails 10x faster using a new pattern-based AI"

This is where "The 3-step formula", "The 7-day method", "The X system" phrases come from. You're not selling the result anymore — you're selling the *mechanism*.

### Stage 4 — Mechanism is old; need a NEW mechanism
The market has now heard several mechanisms. Your mechanism must be **new and different**.

**What works:** Position your mechanism as **contrarian / different / new category**.
- "Forget insulin. It's actually your gut biome that determines weight."
- "Stop using GPT. Use the Anthropic pattern library — it's based on different training data."

### Stage 5 — Market is exhausted; nothing new works
Every claim has been made. Every mechanism has been sold. The audience is numb.

**What works:** **Identification** — make the copy about **who they are**, not what they buy. Sell an identity, a tribe, a lifestyle.

- "For founders who don't do hustle culture"
- "For traders who think technicals are a religion, not a tool"
- "For copywriters who refuse to sound like ChatGPT"

At Stage 5, your buyer buys because of who they become, not what they get.

### The Sophistication–Awareness interaction
Both dimensions matter. A prospect can be:
- **Level 2 (problem-aware) in a Stage 4 (mechanism-tired) market** → needs problem agitation + new mechanism
- **Level 4 (product-aware) in a Stage 1 (fresh) market** → needs direct offer, no need to educate
- **Level 1 (unaware) in a Stage 5 (exhausted) market** → needs story + identity, because direct claims are dead and problem isn't felt

## Part 3 — How to diagnose your prospect's state

Ask these 5 questions before writing any copy:

1. **Do they feel the problem yet?** (No → Level 1. Yes → continue)
2. **Do they know there's a category of solution?** (No → Level 2. Yes → continue)
3. **Do they know your product exists?** (No → Level 3. Yes → continue)
4. **Are they considering but on the fence?** (Yes → Level 4. No → continue)
5. **Are they ready to buy and just need a reason now?** (Yes → Level 5)

Then ask:
6. **How many times has this market heard similar claims?** (0, 1, 2-3, many, exhausted)

These two answers determine:
- Which hook type to use
- Copy length
- Which angle (direct claim, mechanism, identity)
- Which channel
- How much proof
- How much education
- Where to focus the offer

## Part 5 — Most common mistakes

1. **Writing to Level 3 when audience is Level 1.** You show your product; they don't feel a problem yet. No conversion.
2. **Writing to Level 1 when audience is Level 5.** You tell a long story; they just want the deal. They bounce.
3. **Using Stage 1 direct claims in a Stage 4 market.** "Lose 20 lbs!" to a market that's heard this 10,000 times. Invisible.
4. **Using Stage 5 identity copy when market is Stage 2.** "For the brave ones…" when prospects don't even know what category you're in. Confusing.
5. **Running ads to Level 1 audience with product-aware copy.** Meta/Google cold traffic is mostly Level 1 or 2. Your landing page better start at their level, not at "Buy Now".
6. **Treating retargeting like cold traffic.** Retargeting is Level 3-4. Different copy entirely.

## Part 6 — Awareness level → hook type (crossover with Brunson)

From `brunson-hook-story-offer`:

| Awareness | Best hook type |
|---|---|
| 1 Unaware | **Story hook** (backstory, scene setting) |
| 2 Problem-aware | **Problem hook** ("if you're feeling X…") |
| 3 Solution-aware | **Curiosity hook** ("the one thing every X misses") |
| 4 Product-aware | **Proof hook** (case study, testimonial, social proof) |
| 5 Most aware | **Promise hook** (offer, price, deadline) |

## Part 7 — Worked example (for context)

**Selling a $2,000 Gold EA to MT5 traders:**

- **Cold traffic from Meta ads (Level 1-2, Stage 4)**: tell a story about losing $47k on martingale EAs, then reveal the concept of "survivable risk management" without naming the product.
- **Search traffic "best gold ea" (Level 3, Stage 4)**: comparison page showing why every other EA fails on Gold's volatility, then introduce the unique mechanism (e.g., "ATR-adaptive sizing with news-event gating").
- **Retargeting traffic (Level 4)**: case studies with equity curves, guarantee, bonus stack, FAQ handling objections.
- **Email list (Level 5)**: "Cohort closes Friday. New bonus stack just added: [X]."

Same product, four completely different messages.
